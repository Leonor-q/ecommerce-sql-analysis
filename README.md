E-Commerce Sales Analysis
Project Overview

This project analyzes a fictional e-commerce company's sales data using SQL.

The goal is to demonstrate how SQL can be used to transform raw transactional data into meaningful business insights.

The analysis focuses on:

Sales revenue
Product performance
Category performance
Customer purchasing behavior
Monthly sales trends
Average order value
Business insights based on the available data

The dataset is synthetic and was created specifically for this portfolio project.

Business Questions

The analysis answers the following business questions:

How many orders have we received?

Who are the highest-spending customers?

Which products have sold the most units?
Which products have generated the least revenue?
How many units have we sold in each category?
What is the monthly revenue?

What percentage of total revenue comes from each category?

Key Business Insights
Total Revenue

The total revenue generated during the analyzed period was:

€5,618.36

Revenue represents sales generated by the business. It should not be confused with profit, since the dataset does not contain cost information.

Top Revenue Product

Headphones generated the highest revenue:

€959.88

This does not mean that Headphones were the most frequently purchased product in terms of units.

For example, the Notebook sold more individual units, but its lower price resulted in lower total revenue.

This demonstrates the difference between:

Units sold → number of products sold
Revenue → quantity × price

Business Insights

The analysis highlights several relevant business observations.

1. Electronics is the main revenue category

Electronics generated approximately 65.3% of total revenue, making it the largest contributor to sales revenue in the dataset.

This suggests that the company's revenue is strongly concentrated in this category.

However, revenue concentration does not necessarily mean profit concentration because product costs are not available in the dataset.

2. High-priced products can generate more revenue with fewer units

Headphones generated the highest product revenue with €959.88, despite not being the product with the highest number of units sold.

This demonstrates why both sales volume and revenue should be considered when evaluating product performance.

3. September shows an unusual increase in revenue

September generated €1,183.71, making it the highest-revenue month in the dataset.

A possible business hypothesis is that customers may have started preparing for the Christmas shopping season earlier, potentially increasing demand for gift-related products such as electronics.

However, this hypothesis cannot be confirmed using the current dataset.

The analysis therefore follows the principle:

Observation → Investigation → Evidence → Insight

An observed increase in sales should not automatically be treated as proof of its cause.

Open the SQL files located in the sql/ folder and execute the queries in MySQL Workbench.

Basic analysis

This project uses a fictional and relatively small dataset.

The analysis therefore has several limitations:

The dataset does not contain product costs.
Profit and profit margin cannot be calculated.
There is no information about discounts.
There is no information about marketing campaigns.
There is no information about customer acquisition.
The dataset does not contain enough historical data to establish long-term trends.
The dataset cannot confirm the reasons behind changes in monthly revenue.

These limitations are important when interpreting the results.

Possible future improvements include:

Add product cost data and calculate profit margins.
Add discount information.
Add customer acquisition channels.
Add marketing campaign data.
Add more historical sales data.
Build an interactive dashboard using Power BI or Tableau.
